Client Website / Bianco Brothers Manufacturing

Research, Strategy, UX/UI Design, and Launch

Bianco Brothers Manufacturing Website Redesign

Bianco Brothers Manufacturing had built decades of trust through custom commercial seating, but its website no longer reflected the quality, experience, or capabilities of the business. I redesigned and launched a seven-page B2B website that clarified the company's positioning, organized its services and catalog, improved quote and contact pathways, and gave architects, designers, contractors, and dealers a more credible way to evaluate the company online.

Overview

A clearer digital experience for an established commercial manufacturer

Bianco Brothers Manufacturing is an established commercial seating manufacturer serving architects, designers, contractors, dealers, and other commercial buyers. What began as a website redesign became a broader effort to improve how the company presented its services, seating options, experience, and value online.

I used stakeholder input and a competitive audit of 20 companies to reorganize the experience around buyer decisions. The resulting seven-page website clarified Bianco Brothers' positioning, made its capabilities and catalog easier to evaluate, improved access to project imagery and supporting information, and introduced a structured quote workflow that gives the sales team more useful context at first contact.

01 Problem Statement

Decades of manufacturing credibility were getting lost online

Bianco Brothers had built a strong reputation through custom manufacturing, craftsmanship, and long-term client relationships, but its website did not provide visitors with the same level of confidence. The experience was visually dated, difficult to navigate, and unclear about the company's services, seating options, and ability to support custom commercial projects.

This created a gap between the business and the way prospective buyers encountered it online. Architects, designers, contractors, and dealers needed to understand what Bianco Brothers manufactured, evaluate relevant work, compare seating options, and determine how to request a quote. Instead, key information was fragmented or difficult to find, making the company's capabilities harder to assess and adding friction before the first conversation.

The challenge was not simply to modernize the interface. The website needed to translate an established manufacturing operation into a clear digital experience that preserved the company's legacy while making its current capabilities easier to understand, trust, and act on.

02 Goals & Success Criteria

Make Bianco Brothers easier to understand, evaluate, and contact

The redesign needed to do more than make the website look current. It needed to support the questions commercial buyers ask before beginning a conversation: What does Bianco Brothers manufacture? Can the company support my project? What seating and customization options are available? How do I request pricing or discuss a specific requirement?

Evaluation Criteria

  • Navigation
  • Service Clarity
  • Buyer Confidence
  • Quote Readiness

Create a credible buyer experience that supports real commercial decisions

Success meant giving architects, designers, contractors, and dealers a clear path from initial evaluation to service information, catalog access, relevant project imagery, and a better-informed quote request. The experience also needed to reflect the craftsmanship and flexibility behind the business without overwhelming visitors with unnecessary information.

Goal 01

Improve Usability

Create predictable navigation and a clear page structure so visitors could find services, project imagery, catalog information, frequently asked questions, and contact options without unnecessary searching.

Goal 02

Clarify the Capabilities

Distinguish Bianco Brothers' standard seating, custom design, and reupholstery capabilities while clearly communicating who the company serves and what makes its work valuable.

Goal 03

Build Buyer Confidence

Use stronger positioning, detailed product information, real project imagery, and a structured quote workflow to help buyers assess fit and approach the company with greater confidence.

03 Research & Discovery

Understanding why the business was difficult to evaluate online

Discovery focused on two questions: how Bianco Brothers was presenting the business online and what prospective commercial buyers needed before feeling confident enough to begin a conversation.

I reviewed the existing website structure, service information, catalog access, project imagery, company messaging, and inquiry path. I paired that evaluation with stakeholder conversations and a competitive analysis of 20 companies across direct, indirect, and national competitor categories.

The objective was not to copy industry conventions. It was to understand how comparable businesses communicated their capabilities, organized product and service information, established credibility, and guided prospective buyers toward contact or a quote.

The research showed that Bianco Brothers did not lack experience or capability. The problem was that its strongest qualities were difficult to discover and were not organized around the way prospective buyers evaluated a manufacturing partner.

What surfaced during discovery

Key Insights

  • The company's specialization and full range of capabilities were not immediately clear.
  • Standard seating, custom design, and reupholstery services needed clearer separation and explanation.
  • Architects, designers, contractors, and dealers entered the experience with different needs but required a shared understanding of the company first.
  • Project imagery, seating information, and catalog access were valuable forms of proof but were not working together as one evaluation path.
  • Legacy-focused messaging established history but did not adequately communicate the company's current capabilities and flexibility.
  • The existing inquiry process did not collect enough project context to support a productive first conversation.

How the direction was established

Methods

  • Stakeholder discovery and business-priority review
  • Heuristic evaluation of the existing website
  • Content and information architecture audit
  • Competitive analysis of 20 direct, indirect, and national competitors
  • Service, capability, and buyer-audience mapping
  • Catalog, gallery, and product-discovery review
  • Contact and quote-workflow evaluation

04 Key Decisions

Turning research into a clearer buyer journey

The research did not point to a need for more pages, more decoration, or unnecessary features. It showed a need for better organization, stronger proof, and a more useful path from initial interest to a qualified conversation.

Three decisions shaped how the seven-page experience helped prospective buyers understand the company, evaluate its capabilities, and move forward.

Decision 01

Organize the Website Around Buyer Evaluation

The website structure was redesigned around the information prospective buyers needed to make a decision. Services explain what Bianco Brothers can provide, the gallery demonstrates completed work, the catalog supports product discovery, frequently asked questions address common concerns, and contact pathways help visitors take the next step.

This created a more deliberate journey than organizing the website around the company's internal structure alone.

Decision 02

Let the Work Establish Credibility

Bianco Brothers' history remained an important source of trust, but legacy messaging could not carry the entire experience. The redesign paired that history with real project imagery, service explanations, seating options, product details, material information, and examples of the company's craftsmanship.

This allowed prospective buyers to evaluate both the company's experience and its current ability to support commercial projects.

Decision 03

Make the Quote Path Part of the Experience

A generic contact form would not provide enough information for a useful first conversation. The quote workflow was structured to capture seating needs, the buyer's role, project context, and reference-file uploads before the inquiry reached the sales team.

This reduced ambiguity for prospective clients while giving Bianco Brothers more useful information at first contact.

05 Wireframes & Iteration

From structure to refinement

Early exploration focused on the website's architecture before visual styling. I mapped the relationship between the homepage, services, gallery, catalog, frequently asked questions, and contact experience so each page served a distinct purpose within the buyer journey.

Low and mid-fidelity wireframes were used to compare content hierarchy, navigation, service presentation, product discovery, project imagery, and calls to action. The goal was to determine what visitors needed to understand first, what information required its own page, and where buyers would naturally expect to find supporting details.

The homepage evolved into a focused introduction to Bianco Brothers' positioning, capabilities, craftsmanship, and completed work. Service and catalog layouts were refined to distinguish standard seating, custom design, and reupholstery while giving prospective buyers clearer access to product information and relevant examples.

Iteration continued as real content, product details, and project imagery were incorporated. Repeated information was reduced, page relationships became clearer, and contact pathways were positioned around moments when a visitor would be ready to ask a question or request a quote.

The final direction preserved the character and history of the business while creating a more organized, modern, and purposeful commercial experience.

06 Final Design

A clearer, more credible digital presence

The final seven-page website turns Bianco Brothers' experience, capabilities, and craftsmanship into a connected B2B buying experience. Each page supports a specific stage of evaluation while contributing to one consistent understanding of the company.

The homepage establishes the company's positioning, introduces its core capabilities, and uses real project imagery to connect its long-standing reputation with the quality of its current work. Clear navigation gives visitors direct access to services, completed projects, seating options, frequently asked questions, and contact pathways.

Service and catalog experiences distinguish standard seating, custom design, and reupholstery while giving architects, designers, contractors, and dealers the information needed to assess project fit. Product imagery, seating details, available features, and supporting specifications make the company's capabilities easier to compare and understand.

The gallery provides visual evidence of completed work across different commercial environments. Frequently asked questions address common concerns before contact, while the structured quote workflow collects project needs, buyer information, and reference files for a more productive first conversation.

Consistent page hierarchy, responsive layouts, clear calls to action, and a restrained visual system keep the experience focused across desktop, tablet, and mobile devices. The result is a professional website that better represents the business and gives commercial buyers a clearer path from discovery to inquiry.

07 Conclusion

Completed and launched

A stronger path from buyer evaluation to inquiry

The completed seven-page website gives each major buyer task a clear destination, connects the website more closely to the sales process, and makes the company's present-day capabilities easier to understand and evaluate.

What Worked

Clear destinations for major buyer tasks

The completed seven-page website gives each major buyer task a clear destination without making the experience feel unnecessarily large. Services distinguish the company's standard seating, custom design, and reupholstery capabilities, while the gallery, catalog, and frequently asked questions provide different forms of evidence during evaluation.

The structured quote workflow also connects the website more closely to the sales process by collecting project needs, buyer information, and reference files before the first conversation.

What I Would Refine

Let traffic and inquiry data guide the next version

After the website collects enough traffic and inquiry data, I would review which services and seating options receive the most attention, how visitors use the gallery and catalog, where buyers leave the quote process, and whether the frequently asked questions reduce repeated points of confusion.

I would also conduct usability sessions with architects, designers, contractors, and dealers to identify any remaining friction within product discovery, catalog behavior, and quote completion.

What I Learned

History and present-day capability must work together

This project reinforced that redesigning an established business website requires balancing history with present-day capability. Bianco Brothers' legacy created trust, but the experience also needed to demonstrate what the company manufactures today, how it supports commercial projects, and why a prospective buyer should begin a conversation.

It also showed how information architecture, product organization, and inquiry design can support business operations just as directly as visual design. The strongest improvements came from making the company easier to understand, its work easier to evaluate, and the next step more useful for both the buyer and the sales team.
